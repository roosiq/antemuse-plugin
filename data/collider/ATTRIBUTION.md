# Open Collider artifacts — attribution and licence (C3)

The six JSON files in this directory are derived from the Open Collider research
repository. The licensing question is settled; it is not "unverified".

## Upstream licence, as recorded

Pinned source: `CL-ML/open-collider-research`, commit `6ed948a`
(`6ed948ac9e5b5dcc39b82959bbfe1a644e0f7d7f`).

- `LICENSE` at that commit (blob `610e3888`) is MIT **for the source code in
  `script/` only**. Its closing note reads: "This MIT License applies to the source
  code in `script/` only. The dataset, methodology document, blog post, figures, and
  other research artifacts are released under CC BY 4.0 — see `LICENSE-CONTENT.md`."
- `LICENSE-CONTENT.md` at that commit (blob `fa690faa`) releases the non-code
  content under **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)**, and
  lists `OC_data/` (LLM-generated ideas, briefs, reference texts) among the covered
  material. Our artifacts come from `OC_data/creativity_engine/*/prompt.md`.

So the MIT licence does **not** cover these artifacts. CC BY 4.0 does.

The upstream files themselves are stored verbatim in `licensing/LICENSE` and
`licensing/LICENSE-CONTENT.md`, and `licensing/upstream-license.json` records the
commit, the git blob shas, the byte sizes, and the quoted clauses. The stored copies
were read from that commit sha rather than a branch head on 2026-11-07. Nothing here
asks a reader to trust that retrieval: `tests/test_collider.py` recomputes each
stored file's git blob sha and checks it against the record, and checks that every
quoted clause appears in the stored text. That record is a citation, not legal
advice.

## What that requires, and what we do

CC BY 4.0 permits copying, redistribution, remixing, transformation, and building
on the material, including commercially, provided we:

1. give appropriate credit;
2. provide a link to the licence;
3. indicate whether changes were made;
4. do not imply the licensor endorses our use.

Each artifact's `provenance` record carries `license`, `license_url`, and
`attribution`. `core/collider.py` requires all three and rejects a record whose
licence is not `CC-BY-4.0`, whose `license_url` is anything other than the CC BY
link, or whose `attribution` is not the credit line below verbatim. Attribution
therefore cannot silently drop out of a redistributed artifact, and a test pins both
failure modes.

## Changes made to the source material

For each artifact, relative to the cited `prompt.md`:

- `body` is the single numbered entry we used, kept as written. The verbatim match
  against upstream is only checked by the opt-in
  `test_bank_paths_point_at_the_source_that_carries_each_body`, which needs a local
  Open Collider checkout (`ANTEMUSE_OC_REPO`) and skips without one, so it is not
  CI-enforced;
- `mechanism` is a condensation written by us, not upstream text;
- `bridge_question` restates the entry's own question in our voice, and is required
  to appear in `body`;
- `title`, `family`, `id`, and all `provenance` fields are ours;
- `content_hash` is computed over `mechanism` and `bridge_question` only.

## Not verified knowledge

Upstream states that every idea in `OC_data/` is raw LLM output (Claude Sonnet 4)
and that citations inside ideas — case-law references, statute numbers, dates,
named studies, monetary penalties — were generated by the model and have not been
verified. The corpus is published as an artifact of LLM output for methodological
study, not as vetted knowledge.

That is why every artifact here carries `verification_status=generated_example`.
These entries are provocation for a collision, never a factual source. Any factual
claim that reaches a reader must be independently verified.
